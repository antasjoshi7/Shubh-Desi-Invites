Hi, I'm the founder of Shubh Desi Invites. My brother is getting married in February 2027, and this tool started as a way to make his wedding invite.

## How it started

When the family sat down to plan the invites, everyone kept coming back to my cousin's wedding card. It was hand-made: a big arch on each page, Ganesh ji at the top, the couple's names in capitals, and a page for every function, from the Haldi to the reception. My parents wanted exactly that feeling, but as something we could send on WhatsApp.

So I went looking. The free apps put a watermark on the video unless you paid. Others wanted an account before showing a single design, or charged a few hundred rupees per template. Invitation websites cost around ₹1,699 and stopped working after a couple of months. And the big design apps had thousands of templates, but very few that looked like a traditional Indian card, and none of them moved the way a wedding invite should.

## So I built one

I wanted a free tool that puts design and culture first: invites that look like the cards our families actually keep, made for our traditions rather than adapted from generic templates. I tested every change on my phone the way my parents would use it. The designs are painted in the style of printed Indian cards, with a big clear space in the middle so all the details fit. The animations are what I'm proudest of: a baraat ghodi that actually gallops, palace doors that swing open, diyas that light up one by one.

## What I promise

It's free: no watermark, no sign-up, nothing locked behind a payment. Your invite is made on your own phone and never uploaded anywhere, so your family's names and addresses stay with you.
